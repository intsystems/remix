\label{sec:comparison}

For a mixture draw $Z$, write $f=f_\theta(Z)$ and $r_j=r_j(Z)$.
For stratified draws $Z_k$, write $f_k=f_\theta(Z_k)$ and
$\bar f=\sum_k\pi_k f_k$. A component path differentiates the objective
through $T_k$ at fixed noise. The logit and component rows show the

\paragraph*{Sampling budget.}
At a budget of $K$ objective evaluations, stratification draws once per
component. Averaging $K$ independent one-point estimates uses the same
budget, with probability $1-(1-\pi_k)^K$ of drawing from component $k$.

\paragraph*{Variance and runtime.}
Post-stratification averages the selected component's path and weight score
over $C$ conditional on $Z$. With finite second moments, the law of total
variance bounds its variance in each coordinate by that of using the selected
component alone \citep{burroni2026mixture}. Equal objective-evaluation
budgets can yield different runtimes. The implicit estimator, for example,
also pays for the conditional CDF derivatives and the solve.

\paragraph*{Which updates coincide?}
Take two univariate Gaussian components $q_k=\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$
and $f(z)=-z^2/2$. The exact gradient for mean $\mu_k$ is $-\pi_k\mu_k$.
Stratification estimates it by $-\pi_k Z_k$. At the same mixture draw $Z$,
implicit and post-stratified estimation both give $-r_k(Z)Z$, since the
implicit derivative of $Z$ with respect to $\mu_k$ is $r_k(Z)$. For logits,
post-stratification agrees with the zero-baseline score-function estimate for
any objective, since $\partial_{\alpha_j}\log q_\theta(Z)=r_j(Z)-\pi_j$.

\paragraph*{The variational objective.}
For fixed observations $x$ and joint density $p(x,z)$, the evidence lower
bound (ELBO) uses $f_\theta(z)=\log p(x,z)-\log q_\theta(z)$. Its direct
derivative is the negative mixture score. Sticking the Landing (STL)
omits this zero-mean term while preserving derivatives through $z$
\citep{roeder2017stl}. At an exact posterior fit, $f_\theta(z)=\log p(x)$
is constant in $z$. With STL the stratified and implicit updates then
vanish on every draw. The score-function update scales the mixture score
by $\log p(x)-b$, and the post-stratified logit update scales
$r_j-\pi_j$ by $\log p(x)$.
