\section{Comparing the Estimators}
\label{sec:comparison}
Table~\ref{tab:estimators} compares Eqs.~\eqref{eq:score}--\eqref{eq:poststratified}
for a logit $\alpha_j$ and a scalar coordinate $\eta$ of component $\phi_k$.
At a mixture draw $Z$, write $f=f_\theta(Z)$, $h=\nabla_z f_\theta(Z)$,
$r_k=r_k(Z)$, and $s_\lambda=\partial_\lambda\log q_\theta(Z)$ for the score
of either parameter $\lambda$. At a component draw $Z_k$, write
$f_k=f_\theta(Z_k)$ and $h_k=\nabla_z f_\theta(Z_k)$, with
$\bar f=\sum_k\pi_k f_k$. Adding the direct term at fixed points to the
sampling term for $\lambda$ gives the full gradient estimate.

The component velocity $v_k(e)=\partial_\lambda T_k(e;\phi_k)$ holds its
noise fixed. The inverse noise $e_k=T_k^{-1}(Z;\phi_k)$ reproduces $Z$.
For the conditional ranks $S=S_\theta(Z)$ of the implicit estimator,
solve $(D_z S_\theta(Z))^\top u=h$ once for all parameters.
The partial derivatives of $S$ hold $Z$ fixed. In one dimension,
$q=q_\theta(Z)$, $F=F_\theta(Z)$, and $F_k$ is the component CDF at $Z$.

For the variance row, let $G=\partial_\lambda f+h^\top v_C(\varepsilon_C)
+f\,\partial_\lambda\log\pi_C$ be the estimate using the sampled label $C$
and its noise $\varepsilon_C$. We assume finite second moments.

For a fixed model $p(x,z)$ and observations $x$, the evidence lower bound
(ELBO) uses $f_\theta(z)=\log p(x,z)-\log q_\theta(z)$. Sticking the Landing (STL)
drops the zero-mean direct derivative while preserving the sample slope
\citep{roeder2017stl}. At the exact posterior, $f=L=\log p(x)$ and $h=0$.

\paragraph*{Matched sampling budgets.}
With $K$ objective evaluations, stratification samples every component.
Across $K$ independent mixture draws the probability of selecting component
$k$ at least once is $1-(1-\pi_k)^K$.

\paragraph*{Arithmetic cost.}
Evaluating the diagonal Gaussian mixture density costs $O(Kd)$ per point.
The implicit bound assumes $d$ reverse
passes through the $O(Kd)$ rank map to form its Jacobian, followed by an
$O(d^2)$ triangular solve~\citep{burroni2026mixture}.

\paragraph*{Variance.}
Stratification fixes the component counts, so only independent within-component
variances enter its update. Post-stratification averages the single-label
estimate $G$ conditional on $Z$. The law of total variance gives the bound
in the table~\citep{burroni2026mixture}.

\paragraph*{Which updates coincide?}
For two Gaussian components $q_k=\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$,
$\partial_{\mu_k}F_k=-q_k(Z)$. This makes the implicit and post-stratified
mean estimates identical on any given draw for $f(z)=-z^2/2$, with expectation
$-\pi_k\mu_k$. For logits, post-stratification matches the zero-baseline
score-function estimator for any objective.
