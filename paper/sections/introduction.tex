\section{Introduction}
Finite mixtures provide tractable densities with sufficient flexibility to represent
multimodality and heterogeneous posterior uncertainty
\citep{miller2017boosting,morningstar2021mixtures}. Their use in variational inference
requires differentiating expectations with respect to both component parameters and
mixture weights. Exact sampling is straightforward: draw a categorical component
index, then sample its continuous distribution. However, differentiating only the
selected component omits the contribution from changing selection probabilities.

This report specifies four unbiased gradient estimators: a score-function baseline
with the Sticking the Landing (STL) modification, a multi-sample stratified
estimator, implicit reparameterization, and a single-sample post-stratified
estimator \citep{roeder2017stl,figurnov2018implicit,burroni2026mixture}.
We give their assumptions, gradient formulas, and automatic-differentiation
constructions in a common notation. The comparison concerns exact mixture
expectations and distinguishes the number of objective evaluations from the cost
of evaluating all component densities.
