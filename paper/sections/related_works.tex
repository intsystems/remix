\section{Related Work}
Score-function estimators underlie black-box variational inference
\citep{ranganath2014bbvi}; pathwise estimators differentiate a parameterized
transformation of parameter-independent noise
\citep{kingma2014vae,rezende2014backprop}. Their assumptions and variance-reduction
mechanisms are reviewed by \citet{mohamed2020gradients}. For ELBO gradients,
\citet{roeder2017stl} remove the explicit variational-density score term, whose
expectation is zero. This modification preserves unbiasedness but does not imply
a variance reduction for every mixture estimator \citep{burroni2026mixture}.

\citet{graves2016mixture} derives mixture gradients using conditional distribution
functions. \citet{figurnov2018implicit} formulate the general implicit
reparameterization identity, avoiding differentiation of an inverse transformation.
\citet{jankowiak2018transport} connect pathwise gradients to the continuity
equation; \citet{jankowiak2019multivariate} develop multivariate mixture transport
and a hybrid estimator combining pathwise component derivatives with score-based
weight derivatives. The latter is the precursor of the post-stratified estimator
analyzed by \citet{burroni2026mixture}.

Stratified mixture gradients appear in \citet{roeder2017stl} with shared base
noise and in \citet{morningstar2021mixtures} with independent component samples.
The latter also study a stratified importance-weighted objective; here we retain
the ordinary expectation, including the standard ELBO.
\citet{miller2017boosting} instead construct a mixture incrementally by adding
components. Concrete and Gumbel--Softmax relaxations
\citep{maddison2017concrete,jang2017gumbel} replace a discrete selector with a
continuous variable; at finite temperature, gradients of the relaxed objective
are generally biased for the original mixture objective.

An existing PyTorch implementation provides implicit mixture gradients for
factorized components \citep{simkusMixture}. The request for native
\texttt{MixtureSameFamily.rsample()} support is documented in PyTorch issue
\#39178 \citep{pytorch39178}. These software references inform implementation
requirements rather than estimator correctness.
